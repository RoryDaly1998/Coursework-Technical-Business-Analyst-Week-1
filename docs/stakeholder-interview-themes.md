# Stakeholder Interview Themes

## 1. Customer control and self-service

**Common theme:** Many customers may prefer to understand and resolve their accounts themselves when they have clear information and meaningful control. Self-service should be an option, not the only route, and should be designed around customer needs rather than novelty.

- **SN-001:** "Customers respond well when they have control and transparency about what they owe."
- **SN-005:** "Some customers will do anything if given the chance to manage this themselves."
- **SN-017:** "Most customers do not even know they can pay online; they think they have to call."
- **SN-065:** "Customers would pay more readily if they understood exactly what they owe and could see options."
- **SN-081, SN-103:** "If the portal works, it will work because it gives customers control, not because it is fancier."
- **SN-082:** "Self-service works well for straightforward cases, but we need to be honest about which ones those are."
- **SN-099:** "The portal could work brilliantly for the 40% of customers who want to resolve this themselves."
- **SN-109:** "Some customers will do anything if given the chance to manage this themselves."

## 2. Case complexity, triage, and human judgment

**Common theme:** Cases differ in complexity, risk, and customer circumstances. Routine work may be suitable for streamlined handling, but hardship, vulnerability, and other exceptions need clear identification and human judgment.

- **SN-006:** "We have no way to tell whether a customer is genuinely unable to pay or simply avoiding contact."
- **SN-014:** "We cannot tell whether a case failed because of process breakdown or deliberate non-compliance."
- **SN-039, SN-079:** "We do not have a way to identify which cases are straightforward versus which require specialist handling."
- **SN-041:** "We cannot automate the decision about whether a customer is genuinely in hardship or just dodging contact."
- **SN-042:** "We cannot tell whether a case failed because the representative made a mistake or because the customer is genuinely unable to pay."
- **SN-054, SN-121:** "We have edge cases involving hardship, vulnerability, and regulatory forbearance that need human judgment."
- **SN-055:** "Some cases must stay representative-led because risk and specialist rules still apply."
- **SN-057:** "Complex and simple cases get mixed together in the same queue, which slows everything down."
- **SN-069:** "Cases involving vulnerable customers are not flagged differently from routine cases."

## 3. Work prioritisation and contact strategy

**Common theme:** Work is not consistently prioritised by urgency, suitability, or likely opportunity. Stakeholders see value in a more deliberate sequence for handling cases and contacting customers.

- **SN-003:** "Simple cases that could be resolved in minutes take days because they get stuck in the wrong queue."
- **SN-029:** "Straightforward cases get delayed because they are queued behind complex ones with no priority logic."
- **SN-033:** "We could recover an extra 5% of balances if we just contacted people at the right time."
- **SN-037:** "The first contact with a customer is often not optimised because we do not know their full history."
- **SN-045:** "The contact strategy is reactive—we contact people only when collections escalates them."
- **SN-052:** "If we can reduce the time between identifying a payment opportunity and making contact, we win."
- **SN-075:** "Cases pile up on Fridays because no one wants to open new ones before the weekend."
- **SN-077, SN-078, SN-119:** "We reach out to customers in a random order rather than any strategic sequence."
- **SN-100:** "We are treating urgent cases and routine cases the same way, which is why everything feels urgent."

## 4. Follow-up tracking and case continuity

**Common theme:** Callbacks, next steps, and handoffs are inconsistently recorded or owned. This leads to missed follow-ups, repeated contact, and cases that remain stalled without clear accountability.

- **SN-002:** "Customers call back three times because they do not remember what they were told on the first call."
- **SN-007:** "We have cases sitting in 'awaiting callback' status for months because the promised date was never recorded."
- **SN-028:** "Customers go through the contact process multiple times because we have no way to prevent re-contact."
- **SN-038:** "The biggest win will be when representatives stop checking whether work was already done."
- **SN-040, SN-105:** "We lose at least 20% of follow-ups because they fall between shifts and no one owns the handoff."
- **SN-048:** "Reporting takes so long that by the time we see the numbers, they are already out of date."
- **SN-049:** "We have no way to distinguish between a case that is progressing normally and one that is stalled."
- **SN-053, SN-118:** "Cases get stuck in 'pending callback' status indefinitely because the callback date is never enforced."
- **SN-063:** "I cannot tell which customers have already been contacted this month without manually checking three different sheets."
- **SN-032:** "We lose customers because the recovery process is so slow they lose faith we will ever resolve it."
- **SN-036:** "Customers get transferred between departments and have to repeat their entire situation each time."

## 5. Fragmented records, inconsistent data, and audit trail

**Common theme:** Information is spread across systems, duplicated, or recorded inconsistently. This undermines reliable case histories, reporting, handoffs, and the ability to demonstrate what happened.

- **SN-008:** "The audit trail is scattered across email, spreadsheets, and the legacy database, making compliance reviews a nightmare."
- **SN-010:** "A single customer can have five separate records in the system from different entry points."
- **SN-011:** "The collections database does not sync with the email tracker, so representatives often re-contact customers who were already promised callbacks."
- **SN-012:** "The data quality is so poor that we stopped running management reports altogether."
- **SN-015:** "A case marked 'resolved' in one system might be 'in progress' in another."
- **SN-044, SN-104:** "If a customer pays even a small amount, the whole case gets reclassified and we lose months of history."
- **SN-047:** "We are exposed to complaint risk because customers cannot track what we have told them."
- **SN-056:** "Status updates do not capture why a case changed state, only that it did."
- **SN-062:** "Every new tool we add makes the job harder because it adds another place where data can get out of sync."
- **SN-066:** "Audit trails across multiple systems make it nearly impossible to demonstrate what happened to a case."
- **SN-073:** "The activity log does not distinguish between actual contact and failed contact attempts."
- **SN-026:** "We have never measured how often a case comes back because we have no way to track it."
- **SN-067:** "We are losing money on cases that fall through the cracks between systems."
- **SN-070:** "The finance team cannot forecast recovery revenue because they do not trust the activity data."
- **SN-080:** "No one has visibility of what is actually in the pipeline or how long cases typically take."
- **SN-088:** "We have no visibility into why cases are failing; we only know that they do."
- **SN-098:** "I can see that work is happening, but I cannot see whether it is the right work."
- **SN-124:** "We have never measured how often a case comes back because we have no way to track it."
- **SN-085, SN-107:** "There is no standard definition of what each status actually means across the team."
- **SN-111:** "Every status update in the old system requires manual re-keying into the spreadsheet."
- **SN-112:** "The current system treats payment promises the same as payment confirmations, which creates confusion."
- **SN-122:** "Different representatives use different status codes, which makes reporting meaningless."
- **SN-123:** "Every month the finance team has to reconcile our activity count with the database, and they never match."
- **SN-126:** "The current system treats payment promises the same as payment confirmations, which creates confusion."

## 6. Legacy systems, scale, and front-line productivity

**Common theme:** Legacy tools cannot keep pace with current account volumes and product complexity, leaving representatives to rely on manual workarounds. These workarounds obscure costs, slow productivity, and create dependence on individual experience.

- **SN-016:** "The old system was designed for half the volume we handle now, and it shows."
- **SN-021:** "With over 100,000 accounts, we cannot manage this manually any longer, yet that is exactly what we are doing."
- **SN-022:** "The database was designed for a tenth of the volume we handle now, and performance degrades as we approach month-end."
- **SN-023:** "We have more accounts in the system than our representatives can possibly contact in a reasonable timeframe."
- **SN-025:** "The spreadsheet is now two hundred sheets thick and no one knows what half of them do."
- **SN-035:** "The database cannot handle the complexity of modern lending products and customer situations."
- **SN-061, SN-093:** "The 20-year-old system was built for a different business model entirely."
- **SN-086:** "The legacy system was designed before online banking and mobile payments were common."
- **SN-013, SN-106:** "Most of our experienced representatives have learned workarounds that make them faster than any official process."
- **SN-018:** "Some representatives have been doing this for so long they are faster than the system, and they will resist change."
- **SN-020:** "New representatives take two weeks longer to reach productivity because they have to learn the spreadsheet system."
- **SN-027:** "Representatives spend time proving what they did rather than doing more work."
- **SN-034, SN-102:** "Representatives are ready for better tools; they are tired of workarounds."
- **SN-046:** "No one can tell me how much the current workarounds are costing us because the cost is hidden."
- **SN-050, SN-059, SN-116:** "Representatives see solutions that the system does not allow them to implement."
- **SN-068:** "We lose good people because they get frustrated managing around the system instead of managing cases."
- **SN-072, SN-091, SN-114:** "Experienced representatives are faster because they have memorised workarounds that should not be necessary."
- **SN-074:** "If we do not invest in this, we will keep losing good representatives to frustration."
- **SN-087:** "We are paying representatives to hunt for information that should already be on the screen."
- **SN-092:** "The best representatives are being held back by process rather than enabled by it."
- **SN-094:** "I spend more time reading notes from other representatives than I spend actually calling customers."
- **SN-096:** "The spreadsheet workaround cost us five hundred thousand pounds in lost recovery last year."
- **SN-125:** "Our representatives are creative and adaptable; they will find ways to use the portal effectively."
