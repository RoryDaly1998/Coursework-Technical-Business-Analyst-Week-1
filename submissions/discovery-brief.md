# Discovery Brief Template

Use this file to create your one-page discovery brief for Legacy-Trust Bank.

## 1. Problem summary

Write 80-120 words answering:
- What is happening now?
    Right now collections relies on multiple systems and several department specific workarounds. These include spreadsheets, email trails and an outdated database.
- Why is it failing at scale?
    There are too many moving parts and interacting systems. The current system relies on manual handling leading to missed follow ups, duplicated work and inconsistent data recording.
- Why does it matter financially and operationally?
    There is duplication of work leading to inflated staff costs. The current system is impossible to scale as there is a tangle of connected systems. Customer experience suffers due to slow and repetitive processes to record and provide information.

## 2. Stakeholder overview

Complete a short table like the one below.

| Stakeholder group | What they care about | How success is measured | Main worry | Evidence they will trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operations leadership. | Saving reps time. | Workload for reps is reduced. | Automation which complicates the team's work further or adds no benefit. | Clear plan of how automation will save the team time and a process the team can clearly follow. |
| Team leaders and representatives. | That the project makes team members work easier. | Feedback of team members. | The project works great on paper but reps have to step in when problems occur. | Clearly show where rep work begins and ends with the new system. |
| Finance. | Create measurable savings. | Real cost savings realised. | Project doesn't survive real business situations and doesn't save the business money. | Evidence based report of how the project will lead to operational changes and cost savings. |
| Product and delivery. | Project is well thought through and well documented. | Creating a workable prototype. | Poor documentation or unrealistic design. | Fully traceable analysis where statements are always linked to opportunities and buildable requirements. |
| Customers. | Fast, accurate and easy to use service. | Customer feedback and retention. | New system reduces quality of support or security of data. | Using the portal improves their experience. |

## 3. Discovery questions

Write 5-7 questions that point toward evidence.

Starter examples:
- Which steps in debt recovery are high-volume and rules-driven enough for self-service?
- Where do spreadsheets and manual handoffs create duplicate work?
- Which baseline metrics best show operational waste and revenue leakage?

- What are some of the complexities of customer cases that provide the most challenge to reps?
- What information is crucial for the customers to have access to in order to manage their own accounts?
- What current processes are there to maintain data compliance with regulations?
- How many potential users does the new process need to be able to scale for?
- How is the current database structured and what limitations does the 20 year old software have?
- What is the current management structure for handling customer accounts?
- What is the current appeal process for a customer unhappy with how their account has been handled?

## 4. Traceability starter

Create a first-pass table.

| Stakeholder concern | Likely process area affected | Possible metric or evidence source | Likely deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workarounds and legacy tools slow work and obscure case history. | Case management, research and documentation | Time spent per case, rep interviews, rep hours per case solved. | Unified case management system. |
| Missed or unenforced callbacks lead to stalled cases and repeat customer contact. | Follow-up scheduling, case ownership, collections compliance. | Callback completion rate, overdue callbacks, time between promise and follow-up, repeat contacts. | Callback workflow with ownership, dates, and escalation rules. |
| Simple cases are delayed alongside complex cases, and contact order is not strategic. | Triage, queue management, contact prioritisation. | Time to first contact, volume of cases by complexity, resolution time. | Case triage and prioritisation criteria. |
| Inconsistent statuses and fragmented records make reporting and audit difficult. | Case status management, recordkeeping, data compliance. | Status mismatches, incomplete audit records, time to produce reports. | Common status definitions and audit-trail requirements. |
| Customers need clear information and control, but some may not use self-service. | Customer contact, payment and account management, assisted service. | Self-service take-up, task completion and abandonment, repeat contacts, customer satisfaction surveys. | Customer journey options for self-service and assisted support. |
| Hardship, vulnerability, disputes, and regulatory forbearance require human judgment. | Case assessment, handling special circumstances. | Exception volumes, review outcomes, complaints or compliance incidents. | Human-review triggers and exception paths. |
| The financial case must show real benefit without shifting costs or harming recovery. | Finance reporting, recovery operations, benefits tracking. | Cost per case, representative time, self-service impact on workload and recovery. | Hard savings and forecasted revenue kept separately with assumptions clearly stated. |
| Representatives may resist changes or rely on learned workarounds. | Training, day-to-day case handling. | Representative feedback, training time, adoption and workaround rates, productivity over time. | Representative engagement, training, and rollout plan |

## 5. Final problem statement

Legacy-Trust Bank need a service which allows customers to complete basic account functions in a way which reduces the workload of human representatives. There needs to be a better and more unified system for handling customer cases and documentation needs to be clearer and easier to access.
